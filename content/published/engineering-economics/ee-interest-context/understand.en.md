## From the situation to a defensible decision

This is a course-specific theory chapter, not a factor table. Learn the lecture’s classification and reasoning through concrete transactions, while keeping mathematical equivalence separate from a normative judgment.

### 1. Separate a calculation from a judgment

The second chapter frames economic systems through their treatment of interest. It presents a religious and ethical argument, while later chapters calculate interest-based equivalence as an analytical method. A numerical model can describe what a contract demands without declaring that contract permissible. When answering the theory exam, identify the lecturer’s position, define the transaction and explain the stated reason. Do not turn the lecture’s historical political or wealth-distribution claims into unqualified current statistics.

```text
description of cash flows ≠ normative approval
```

**What each symbol means** — A mathematical rate measures a specified change per period; a normative conclusion concerns the transaction and its governing framework.

**Try this situation** — A statement describes 1000 CU now and 1100 CU in one year.

**Follow the calculation** — The stipulated increase is 100 CU, or 10% of principal. That arithmetic alone is not a ruling on the transaction.

**What the result changes** — For this chapter, understanding includes knowing what is a definition, a value judgment or an empirical claim.

**When this model applies** — This journey summarizes the supplied lecture’s viewpoint for study; legal/religious classifications must be attributed to that source.

### 2. Riba al-nasi’ah: inspect the loan condition

The lecture describes riba al-nasi’ah as a stipulated benefit linked to a loan and its delay. It explicitly includes small and large increases and consumption and investment loans; the label is not restricted to very high compounding. Identify what was lent, what must be returned, the time and any stipulated benefit. The lecture also discusses non-cash benefits. Keep that description attributed rather than inventing a threshold from the size of a percentage.

```text
stipulated monetary excess = repayment − principal
```

**What each symbol means** — Principal and repayment are currency amounts. A non-cash contractual benefit requires a description, not a fabricated cash amount.

**Try this situation** — A lecture scenario lends 100 CU with a stipulated repayment of 101.

**Follow the calculation** — The stipulated excess is 1 CU. The lecture’s distinction is the condition, not whether the excess looks small.

**What the result changes** — Classify the contractual condition before discussing the use of the funds.

**When this model applies** — Apply the classification stated in chapter 2; do not infer a modern contract’s ruling from an incomplete one-line scenario.

### 3. Riba al-fadl: quantity, kind and delivery

For the specified ribawi exchanges in the lecture, price equality is not a substitute for the required quantity and delivery conditions. Its date-quality example recommends selling for money and then buying, rather than directly exchanging unequal quantities of the same category. The exam cases distinguish same-kind commodities from different-kind commodities and immediate delivery from delay. Explain which condition is being tested instead of memorizing a yes/no list detached from the transaction.

```text
same specified kind: equal amount + immediate exchange (lecture classification)
```

**What each symbol means** — Kind, measured amount and delivery time are separate variables; commercial price is a fourth variable.

**Try this situation** — Two bags of ordinary wheat are exchanged now for two equal-measure bags of better wheat now.

**Follow the calculation** — The lecture’s example accepts equal amount and immediate exchange despite different quality. If 3 are exchanged for 2 of the same kind with delay, both amount and timing conditions fail.

**What the result changes** — Test quantity and delivery independently and name the source’s reason.

**When this model applies** — This is the source’s simplified commodity framework; do not generalize it to every modern instrument or commodity.

### 4. Build a delivery matrix for the contract types

Instead of five disconnected names, imagine two clocks: one for the price and one for delivery of the good. The lecture lists immediate exchange, deferred payment with current delivery, and salam with current payment and specified future delivery. It also criticizes both sides being deferred and transactions with unclear subject or price. The matrix helps recall its categories, but does not replace the conditions of a fully specified contract.

```text
contract description=(price timing, goods timing, specification)
```

**What each symbol means** — Timing may be now or a stated future date; specification concerns what and how much is promised.

**Try this situation** — A farmer receives 600 CU now for a specified future crop delivery.

**Follow the calculation** — The source places current payment/future specified delivery in salam. Current goods/future price reverses that timing.

**What the result changes** — A positive financial metric and a valid contractual description answer different questions.

**When this model applies** — Use the lecture’s terminology; some category names are presented differently in other legal traditions.

### 5. Explain the lecturer’s criticisms as arguments

The lecture groups alleged consequences of interest into social, distributional, moral, economic and political criticisms. Its examples include concentration of wealth, pressure from debt, risky behavior and monopoly. Learn the causal chain being proposed: a fixed creditor claim, uncertainty in the productive outcome, and who bears the downside. For a theory answer, attribute the argument and give a concrete mechanism. A slide’s sweeping conclusion is not an independently established universal causal law.

```text
cash remaining after debt = realized operating cash − contractual payment
```

**What each symbol means** — Realized cash and the contractual payment have currency units for the same date.

**Try this situation** — Operating cash turns out to be 80 CU; a fixed payment of 110 is due.

**Follow the calculation** — 80−110=−30 CU. The shortfall illustrates how a fixed claim interacts with uncertain business receipts.

**What the result changes** — Attribution preserves course coverage without presenting every slide claim as settled fact.

**When this model applies** — A hypothetical one-period illustration of the lecture’s mechanism, not a general empirical proof or investment recommendation.
